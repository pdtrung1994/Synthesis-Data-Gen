Question 1 (Q1): Baseline Performance & Hyperparameter Tuning Gains

How do machine learning models perform across different initial seed sizes (1, 2, 5, 10, 20) before and after classifier hyperparameter tuning (Baseline vs. Tuned Baseline)?

  • Substantial Tuning Gains for RNN (Seed Size ≥ 2): With default parameters, RNN achieves an overall mean accuracy of 49.17%. Classifier hyperparameter tuning lifts RNN accuracy to 51.86% (+9.71%) at Seed = 2, 58.63% (+14.41%) at Seed = 5, and 69.53% at Seed = 20.
  • One-Shot Regime (Seed Size = 1): KNN achieves the highest 1-shot accuracy at 70.65% (using 1-nearest neighbor), while RNN remains at 41.23%.
  • Strong Traditional Baselines: KNN (mean 82.89%) and RandomForest (mean 80.86%) provide the highest baseline accuracy on unaugmented data, exceeding 93% at Seed = 20.

Question 2 (Q2): Effect of Synthetic Data on Machine Learning Models

How does each synthetic data generation method impact different ML classifiers relative to the Default Baseline (Q2.1) and the Tuned Baseline (Q2.2)?

  • Major Improvements on RNN: Synthetic data augmentation improves RNN accuracy by +12.77% to +20.31% over Default Baseline (all generators except TimeVAE, which gains +5.97%), and provides a net gain of +10.48% (Mixup), +10.45% (GMM), +10.10% (SMOTE), and +8.99% (Time_Warping) above Tuned Baseline.
  • Consistent Gains Across All Five Classifiers: Six methods (Jittering, Scaling, Magnitude_Warping, SMOTE, Mixup, GMM) deliver positive mean net gains (ΔAcc > 0) on all five classifiers compared to Tuned Baseline. Jittering leads on RandomForest (+1.98%), LinearSVC (+1.68%) and LogisticRegression (+0.73%), GMM leads on KNN (+0.99%), and Mixup leads on RNN (+10.48%). For the traditional classifiers these gains are small (mostly below 2%).
  • Few-Shot Limitations of TimeVAE: Training a variational autoencoder on very small seed sets (2–20 samples per class) causes overfitting, slightly helping RNN (+0.30% vs. Tuned Baseline) but reducing accuracy on LinearSVC (-6.70%) and LogisticRegression (-5.29%).
Note on generator-configuration selection: Q2–Q4, Q6 and Q7 use the best generator configuration per (Dataset, Model, Seed Size, Scale Factor), selected by mean F1 on the same test folds that are used for evaluation. These figures are therefore an optimistic upper bound. If results are instead averaged over all tested configurations, the net gain over Tuned Baseline on RNN stays clearly positive for 8 of 10 generators (+6.2% to +9.4%; HMM_GMM −2.2%, TimeVAE −7.0%), whereas on KNN, LinearSVC, LogisticRegression and RandomForest most gains shrink to within ±1.5% and often turn negative. Mixup is the only generator that stays positive on all five classifiers under this averaging.

Question 3 (Q3): Scaling Effect by Seed Size (1x to 10x)

How does expanding synthetic data volume from 1x (seed baseline) up to 2x, 3x, 5x, and 10x affect accuracy across seed sizes?

  • Continuous Scaling Growth on RNN: For Seed ≥ 2, RNN accuracy increases steadily from 1x through 10x and exceeds the Tuned Baseline from 2x onward for every generator. At Seed = 1, accuracy dips slightly at 2x–3x and only rises clearly at 5x–10x (to about 50–51%).
  • Early Saturation (2x–3x) on Traditional Classifiers: For KNN, LinearSVC, LogisticRegression, and RandomForest, most accuracy gains are achieved at 2x–3x scaling and remain stable through 10x.
Note on the ADASYN Curve at Seed Size = 10 and 20: ADASYN only generates synthetic samples when classes overlap near decision boundaries. Across the 5 datasets, ADASYN only produces samples on the 2 harder datasets with overlapping boundaries (Chinese_wine and Coffee at Seed = 10 and 20). Consequently, the ADASYN curve averages over those 2 harder datasets (where RNN 1x baseline is 39.11% at Seed 10 and 49.08% at Seed 20), whereas the other 9 methods average across all 5 datasets (54.41% at Seed 10 and 63.78% at Seed 20). Within those 2 datasets, ADASYN strongly improves RNN accuracy from 39.11% (1x) → 65.29% (10x) at Seed 10 and 49.08% (1x) → 65.82% (10x) at Seed 20.

Question 4 (Q4): Effect of Seed Size per Generator Method

How does initial seed volume (1, 2, 5, 10, 20) influence method applicability and scaling trajectories from 1x to 10x?

  • Single-Sample Augmentations (Jittering, Scaling, Magnitude_Warping, Time_Warping): Operate at all seed sizes including Seed = 1, lifting 1-shot RNN accuracy from 41.23% to 50.2%–51.3% at 10x (best: Time_Warping, 51.32%).
  • Multi-Sample Generators: SMOTE, Mixup, GMM and HMM_GMM produce results only from Seed ≥ 5; TimeVAE only at Seed = 20; ADASYN only at Seed = 10 and 20, and only on 2 of the 5 datasets (Chinese_wine, Coffee). Where available, SMOTE, Mixup and GMM show smooth upward trajectories.

Question 5 (Q5): Generator Hyperparameter Sensitivity

How sensitive is classification accuracy to generator hyperparameter choices (5 configurations for 1-parameter methods, 10 configurations for multi-parameter methods)?

  • Most Stable Generators: Mixup (spread of only 0.16%, best at alpha = 0.8 with 82.57% accuracy), SMOTE (spread 0.29%, best at k_neighbors = 5 with 82.42%), and Jittering (spread 0.71%, best at sigma = 0.05) are highly robust to parameter selection.
  • Parameter-Sensitive Generators: Signal-warping methods (Scaling, Magnitude_Warping, Time_Warping) require small perturbation magnitudes (sigma = 0.05); larger perturbations distort spectral peaks and reduce accuracy by 3.90%–4.44%. For GMM, the best configuration is n_components = 2, covariance_type = 'tied' (82.51%); tied/full covariance configurations are nearly identical (82.37%–82.51%), while diag/spherical covariances perform worst (79.18%–81.82%).

Question 6 (Q6): Statistical Significance Testing (Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test)

Are accuracy gains over Tuned Baseline statistically significant (p < 0.05) across paired folds?

  • RNN: 9 out of 10 generators (all except TimeVAE) achieve statistically significant gains (p < 0.0001) over Tuned Baseline, with win rates between 66.5% (HMM_GMM) and 91.8% (Mixup).
  • Traditional Classifiers: Top generators (GMM, Mixup, SMOTE, Jittering) achieve statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) on KNN, LinearSVC, and RandomForest (on RandomForest, Jittering reaches an 85.8% win rate and GMM 78.0%). Note that per-case gains for these classifiers are small (<2%), and p-values treat Scale Factors and Seed Sizes as separate paired observations, so they are not fully independent.

Question 7 (Q7): Computational Cost vs. Accuracy Gain

What is the efficiency trade-off between execution time per fold (seconds) and net accuracy gain over Tuned Baseline?

  • Pareto-Optimal Generators: Jittering (1.87 s/fold, +2.50% net gain) and Magnitude_Warping (1.78 s/fold, +2.24% net gain) are the fastest high-gain methods and support 1-shot learning. For Seed ≥ 5, GMM (2.63 s/fold, +2.75% net gain, 83.10% mean accuracy) and Mixup (2.49 s/fold, +2.61% net gain, 82.96% mean accuracy) deliver the highest overall accuracy.
  • Caveat: Mean accuracy is not directly comparable across generators, because methods cover different seed sizes (e.g., GMM/Mixup/SMOTE start at Seed = 5, TimeVAE only at Seed = 20, while Jittering includes the hardest Seed = 1–2 cases). Net gain vs. Tuned Baseline is the fairer comparison.
